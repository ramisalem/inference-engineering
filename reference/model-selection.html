Model selection

How to pick a model and prove the pick, on one page.

The rule

Pick "the smallest, easiest-to-run model that's smart enough to handle the task at hand".Baseten, Inference Engineering §1.3 "Model selection". "Smart enough" is decided by your eval, not a leaderboard.

Checklist

  1. Write down the job. Task kinds, languages, latency target (p50 and p95), volume, budget, data rules.
  2. Shortlist 2–4 candidates from public benchmarks (for example Artificial Analysis). Prefer popular architectures, since tooling supports them first.
  3. Read the licenses. Open weights still have terms.
  4. Build an eval from real tasks. Start with 10–20, grow to 50+. Temperature 0, fixed seed.
  5. Run it. Record pass rate, E2E p50/p95, TTFT, output tokens and $/1k requests.
  6. Read every failure: model, prompt or check?
  7. Decide, and say what would change your mind.

Shared vs dedicated

Shared (per token)Dedicated (per GPU-hour)
You pay fortokens usedhours reserved, busy or idle
Good whenbefore product-market fit; spiky, small or unknown trafficlarge, steady traffic; strict latency, privacy or custom models
Typical fitconsumer apps (cost-sensitive, unpredictable)B2B (stable usage, latency and uptime matter)
break-even tokens/month = GPU $/hour × 720 ÷ shared $ per token
needed average throughput = break-even tokens ÷ 2,592,000 s

Example (made-up prices): $2/h → $1,440/month; at $0.50 per million tokens the break-even is 2,880 M tokens/month, or ~1,111 tok/s around the clock, at your latency target. Engineering time pushes the real break-even higher.

Cost per request

$/1k requests = (input tok × $in + output tok × $out) ÷ 1,000,000 × 1,000

Thinking tokens are output tokens: decoded at ITL, holding a slot, billed at the output price. With thinking, TTFT measures the first thought, so also track the time to the first answer token.

Memo template (one page)

  1. Use case and constraints: the job, latency target, volume, budget, data rules.
  2. Candidates and why they were shortlisted.
  3. Eval: how many tasks, what kinds, which checks, settings.
  4. Results: the table.
  5. Recommendation.
  6. Why not the others.
  7. Risks, and what would change my mind.
  8. Next step.
